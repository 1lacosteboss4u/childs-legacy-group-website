Frequently Asked Questions

Questions the family should be able to answer clearly.

These answers summarize the current planning framework. Final legal, tax, securities, investment, estate and operating rules depend on professional review and executed documents.

What is Child's Legacy Group?

A proposed multigenerational family wealth and education system organized around the ten original family branches.

Is it legally formed yet?

No legal formation is represented by this website. Proposed entities and structures remain planning concepts until formally created and confirmed.

Why $25 per week?

The current model uses 52 scheduled contribution periods × $25 = a $1,300 annual target per participating member. It is an illustration, not an active subscription.

Can members withdraw during Years 1–5?

The current foundation-period proposal does not allow routine withdrawals, personal loans or routine distributions.

Are returns guaranteed?

No. Investments can gain or lose value, dividends can change, businesses can fail and real estate can lose money.

What happens after Year 5?

Approved distributions may be considered only from Available Distributable Cash after expenses, taxes, reserves, debt, working capital and future commitments.

What are Legacy Units?

Legacy Units are a planning concept discussed in family documents. They are not legally issued interests yet.

How do the Ten Branches work?

The current governance proposal gives each original branch one Council seat, with a Representative and Alternate.

What if someone misses contributions?

Under the current planning baseline, missed contributions would not erase prior economic interests; any catch-up would use then-current approved valuation rules.

What happens after death or incapacity?

Ownership records should be preserved and handled through authorized estate, trust, court or agent processes—not informal family decisions.

Can family businesses receive funding?

Possibly, but being family does not automatically qualify a business. Every proposal should meet consistent underwriting standards.

Who controls the money?

The proposed model separates initiation, approval, execution, bookkeeping, reconciliation and reporting so no one person controls the whole chain.

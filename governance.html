Governance before growth

Ten branches. Ten seats. Distributed control.

The current family-proposed governance baseline gives each original branch one Council seat while keeping economic ownership separate from governance rights.

Council architecture

Representation without concentrated power.

Each branch selects a Representative and Alternate. Economic participation does not buy extra Council votes, and Council service does not automatically create economic ownership.

Representative + Alternate

Each original branch has one voting seat and one backup representative for continuity and conflict replacement.

Governance ≠ ownership

Voting voice and economic interest are tracked separately so money alone does not control family governance.

No chair tie-breaker

A 5–5 vote fails. No permanent unilateral financial authority is assigned to one chair or founder.

Voting ladder

Bigger decisions require stronger consensus.

These thresholds are current planning rules and remain subject to final legal structure and governing documents.

6/10Ordinary

Routine Council-level decisions.

7/10Major

Significant institutional or financial decisions.

8/10Foundational

Constitutional or permanent-structure changes.

9/10Extraordinary

Near-unanimous structural decisions.

Money protections

Initiate → Approve → Execute → Reconcile → Report.

The operating model separates roles so mistakes and misuse are harder to hide and easier to detect.

1Initiate

Document the request, amount, purpose and supporting evidence.

2Approve

Independent review checks policy, limits, conflicts and required votes.

3Execute

Carry out only the action that was actually approved.

4Reconcile

Match books, receipts and external statements independently.

5Report

Provide consistent, documented family reporting and audit trail.

Dual control: external withdrawals and bank payments should require two authorized people. Reserved transactions—such as distributions, real-estate acquisitions, family-business funding, new debt, new accounts, signer changes and related-party transactions—require stronger Council review.
Approval Panel

Independent review for sensitive actions.

The proposed panel uses three Council members from different branches, rotating terms, conflict recusal and separation from bookkeeping, reconciliation and sole execution roles.

3 different branches

Approval authority is distributed across separate branch representatives rather than concentrated in one office.

Rotating service

Normal one-year terms and limits on consecutive service reduce permanent personal control.

Conflict replacement

A conflicted person does not approve their own related transaction; an authorized replacement steps in.

Lesson 01

Investing Basics

Investing means putting money into an asset with the expectation that it may create income, grow in value, or both. Unlike emergency cash, invested money can fall in value.

What it means

Stocks represent ownership, bonds generally represent lending, real estate can create rent and equity, and businesses can create profit and enterprise value.

Why it matters

Near-term money needs liquidity. Long-term money can accept more volatility because it has more time to recover and compound.

Key risk

Nothing eliminates risk. The goal is to understand, diversify, budget, monitor and manage it instead of pretending it does not exist.

Lesson 02

Stocks vs. ETFs

One stock depends heavily on one company's results. A broad ETF can spread money across hundreds or thousands of companies, reducing company-specific risk.

Important: not every ETF is broadly diversified. Sector and thematic ETFs can still be concentrated and volatile.
Lesson 03

Dividends

A dividend is a payment a company chooses to make to shareholders. It can add to total return and cash flow, but boards can increase, reduce, suspend or eliminate dividends.

Income

Dividends can be received as cash or reinvested into additional shares.

Yield

Dividend yield compares annual dividend payments with share price, but a high yield can sometimes signal distress.

Reinvestment

Reinvesting income can increase the number of shares owned over time, but the investment can still lose value.
